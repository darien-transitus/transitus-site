Hoping Colorado emergency mortgage help is real

Wishful this program saves your home.

Written by Transitus · Denver, Colorado · September 6, 2026

Someone handed you four letters. A counselor said them, or a servicer letter did, or a stranger on a forum who swore it saved their house. EMAP. Colorado emergency mortgage assistance program. You have been holding those letters like a ticket you have not dared to check, because the missed months are real, the regular payment could work again if the gap were closed, and this sounded like the one thing built to close it. I will not make you wait on the answer. The program was real. It is closed now. I am going to stay with you past that sentence, because the end of one program is not the end of the help.

It existed for people exactly like you, and it ran out

Colorado's Emergency Mortgage Assistance Program was the state's share of a federal pool, the Homeowner Assistance Fund. It was created by the American Rescue Plan and spread across roughly $10 billion of programs nationwide. In Colorado it did precisely what you are hoping for. It helped income-qualified homeowners hurt by the pandemic get caught up on past-due mortgage payments, property taxes, HOA dues, homeowners insurance, lot rent on a mobile home, even utilities. A catch-up, with the state's name on it to help you.

The catch is the calendar. EMAP was built as a temporary answer to COVID. It opened in October 2021, and applications were reviewed in the order they came in until the money was gone. As of September 2026, the Division of Housing says every available dollar has been spent and it cannot provide additional assistance. The federal fund behind it was scheduled to end this same September regardless. So this is not a waitlist, and it is not a program pausing between rounds. It is finished.

I know how that lands. You did the hard part, letting yourself hope out loud, and the answer is that the help ran out before you reached the front of the line. That is not a verdict on you. It is timing, and timing was never in your hands. But what you still have is your agency to push forward and many more options to keep your home.

The state still answers the phone, it just does not write the check

Colorado still has people to help. The state's CARE Center is the door the Division of Housing now points to. It connects homeowners with housing counseling, legal assistance, and other services aimed at keeping you in the home. You can call or text 720-356-0174, email info@carecenter.us, or chat at cedproject.org, Monday through Friday, 8:30 to 5:30.

There is also the Colorado Foreclosure Hotline at (877) 601-4673, and HUD-approved housing counselors, who do foreclosure prevention counseling free of charge and answer the HOPE Hotline at (888) 995-4673 at any hour of any day. The federal consumer bureau's advice for someone whose state program has run out of money is exactly that counselor. And if the missed months have pulled other things under with them, 2-1-1 Colorado connects people with food, rental help, and local emergency resources, confidentially and in more than one language.

None of that is money for the past-due pile. I won't dress it up. But these are the people whose job is to find whatever does still exist for your exact situation.

The catch-up you were looking for lives somewhere else now

The plainest thing I can tell you. The way most people close a gap like yours, is through the company you send your payment to. The federal consumer bureau's standing guidance for anyone who cannot pay is to call the servicer right away, and the options a servicer can put on the table are shaped for exactly this. To show you ways that you can still keep your home even in the tough spot you are in.

I know calling the people you owe feels like turning yourself in. For once, the law has your back on this. Before a foreclosure can even be filed in Colorado, they have to mail you a notice carrying the hotline number you already have and the direct number of their own loss mitigation department (CRS 38-38-102.5(2)). The desk for this conversation is already set up. And you do not have to sit at it alone. The counselors above sit on your side of the table for exactly this call.

One guardrail, because the word assistance program attracts people who are not the state. It is illegal in Colorado for a foreclosure consultant to charge you an up-front fee (CRS 6-1-1107). Real help here is free. Anyone charging to get you into state money that no longer exists has just told you who they are.

The hope was not the mistake

There was hope EMAP could help you. You still believe this house can be carried, and nothing about a closed program says you were wrong. The help that was supposed to save you ran dry and that is heavy. But it’s not the end. If you want to see all your options, we at Transitus can show you when you're ready.
