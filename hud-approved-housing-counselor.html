Hoping a HUD approved housing counselor is real

Afraid to believe the free foreclosure help is real.

Written by Transitus · Denver, Colorado · August 8, 2026

There are two of you reading this. One is hoping, maybe for the first time in weeks, that a real person exists whose whole job is helping you through this, for free. The other has seen enough of what your situation attracts to hear the word free the way you would hear it from a stranger knocking at dinner time. Both halves of you are right to be here.

So let me not make you wait: yes. The free help is real. A HUD approved housing counselor is a real person, foreclosure prevention counseling is their actual job, and it truly costs you nothing.

A person whose job is to be on your side

Strip away the clunky government title and here is what a HUD approved housing counselor actually is: a person. Someone trained for exactly the hardship you are in. Who sits down with your real numbers and your real letters and helps you see what your options actually are. These counselors work through agencies HUD approves and oversees as part of a national network, so this is not a company that found your name on a list. It is a program built for homeowners in trouble, and the foreclosure prevention counseling it provides is free of charge. Not free-for-the-first-call free. Not free-until-the-paperwork free. Just free.

The numbers that reach a real person

Let’s start with the numbers. The HOPE Hotline at (888) 995-4673 connects you with HUD approved housing counseling, and it is open 24 hours a day, seven days a week. Which means the 2 a.m. version of you, the one doing this search while the house is quiet, does not have to wait until morning to hear a voice.

HUD itself can point you to an agency near you at 800-569-4287, and the CFPB has a Find a Counselor tool online. Here in Colorado, there is also the Colorado Foreclosure Hotline at (877) 601-4673, a resource the state's own courts point homeowners to. You were picturing one overwhelmed office somewhere. Luckily, it is a roster of potential support.

The catch you keep waiting for

I know you may be wondering: what do they get out of it? Where is the bill hiding? That instinct has protected you before, so I am not going to talk you out of it. I am just going to show you something a scammer could never survive.

Under federal rules, by the 45th day you are behind, your own servicer has to send you a written notice that includes where to find HUD counselors and the toll-free number to reach them (12 CFR 1024.39). And in Colorado, before a foreclosure can even be filed, the notice mailed to you at least thirty days ahead has to carry the Colorado foreclosure hotline's number (CRS 38-38-102.5(2)).

So, the companies you owe money to are legally required to hand you the phone number of the people who will help you deal with them. No matter how strange that sounds it’s the truth. The federal consumer bureau says it as plainly as I ever could: you do not have to pay anyone to help you avoid foreclosure. The catch you keep bracing for is not here.

Honest about what they cannot do

Now the honest part, because a real yes has one and a fake yes never does. A housing counselor cannot promise you will keep your home. No one can honestly promise you that, and the real ones do not try. Their job is to help you look at the options you have and decide what fits your life, not to guarantee an ending.

I know part of you wanted the person on the phone to say they will fix it. But hold onto this instead: the fact that they will not promise you the moon is one of the ways you know they are real.

Telling real free from fake free

Here is the brightest line Colorado draws between real and fake. It is illegal for anyone acting as a foreclosure consultant to charge you an up-front fee (CRS 6-1-1107). And since you know the real thing is free, anyone who breaks from that has just told you who they are. So please walk away.

Beyond that, the warning signs the federal consumer bureau lists are simple. Someone guaranteeing they will get your mortgage terms changed. Someone asking you to sign over the title, or to sign anything you do not understand. Someone telling you to send payments to them instead of your servicer, or to stop paying altogether. A HUD approved housing counselor does none of those things. That is how you tell if a person is on your side from one that isn’t.

You were right to hope

You typed that search half sure the answer would be one more disappointment. It is not. The person you hoped exists does exist, and reaching out is not how you get taken advantage of. It is how you stop carrying this alone. And we at Transitus can show you other options to avoid foreclosure. You asked if anyone helps. Yes they do. Let someone, and put this behind you.
